Three steps to a lost page

  1. Enter a URL

    Paste a page address to search for that page, or a site address to search more broadly.

  2. Scan the archive

    Crawlback checks Common Crawl's index for saved versions, with their capture dates. An empty result does not prove a page never existed.

  3. View snapshots

    Open matching snapshots, check them against other sources, and save the results if you want.

An archive that stays out of your way

  • Alternative archive

    Finds snapshots the Wayback Machine can miss, including moments it never captured.

  • Local-first

    Searches run from your browser, and your history stays there with no server-side storage. You can wipe everything from Settings.

  • Sandboxed viewing

    Archived HTML renders in a sandbox. No archived scripts execute, nothing phones home.

  • Portable results

    Export capture lists as CSV or JSON, or download raw HTML as ZIP or WARC to keep.

Built on Common Crawl

An independent nonprofit has been crawling the web since 2008 and publishing everything as open data.

It collects samples of the web, so it does not save every page or capture every change. Crawlback uses those crawls to find individual pages inside the archive. Results are captured HTML, not live replays.

Frequently asked

How is this different from the Wayback Machine?+

The Wayback Machine archives on demand and replays full pages. Crawlback searches Common Crawl, a separate archive built by broad crawls since 2008. The two overlap only partially. Common Crawl often holds captures the Wayback Machine is missing, which is exactly when Crawlback is useful.

Why can a scan take so long?+

Common Crawl limits request rates, and everyone on the same network can share one public IP address and its limit. Crawlback slows down on purpose to avoid getting your search blocked, so some scans take 20 minutes or more.

Why is the preview incomplete?+

Common Crawl mostly stores the captured HTML, not the images, stylesheets, and scripts that were loaded with it. Crawlback shows the captured HTML in a sandbox and loads nothing from the original site, so opening a capture does not reveal your visit.

How should I verify a capture before publishing?+

Keep the exact URL and the capture date. The capture date is when Common Crawl saved the page, not when it was first published. Compare the capture with other captures and independent sources.

Does a scan hit the target website?+

Almost never. Archive searches go straight to Common Crawl. Crawlback's server makes one request to the target, a robots.txt check, which you can turn off in Settings.
